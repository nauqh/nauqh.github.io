## How to adapt

**Every application:**

- Fill `[Company]`, `[Role]`, `[Hiring Manager]` (use "Hiring Team" if no name found).
- The `[hook line about the company]` in paragraph 1: one specific line - a product, an engineering blog post, a stated value. Never skip it; it's the only sentence proving the letter was written for them. If their work doesn't fit the "products inside a real business" framing, rewrite the clause after the colon to match what they actually do.
- Hiring managers decide in ~6 seconds whether to keep reading, so the opener carries the letter. Never revert to "I am writing to apply for…".

**Alternative hooks** - swap the first sentence if the default opener feels off for the posting:

> *Metric-first:* "Two hours to fifteen minutes - that's what happened to customer response times at Tierra after I shipped their AI support system."
>
> *Company-first (when you have a strong research hook):* "[Company]'s [specific product/post] tackles the problem I've spent the last two years shipping solutions for: making AI systems useful inside real businesses, not just in demos."

**AI Engineer postings** - keep as-is. Optionally swap the final Tierra sentence ("Shipping it meant…") for a technical one:

> I designed the full pipeline - embedding strategy, retrieval tuning, and prompt orchestration via LangChain and n8n - and iterated on it against live conversation logs.

**FDE postings** - keep the Tierra closer as-is (it is the FDE signal). The opener already frames deployment-inside-a-business, so no paragraph 1 change needed.

**If the posting emphasises scale/infra** - expand the learning platform sentence: add the AWS S3 content pipeline and incremental sync via MD5 hashing across 400+ lesson files.

**Word count:** ~330 as written (body only) - inside the 250–400 sweet spot from hiring-manager surveys. If a form caps at 250, cut the Zoho sentence and the learning-platform sentence first; keep the hook intact.
